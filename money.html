How public money actually moves

There is no “motorcycle circuit grant” sitting in a drawer.

Large motorsport money almost never arrives as a sport grant. It arrives as economic development, tourism, a major-event leftover, or an election promise. Ordinary sport grants top out at $750,000 and need a matching contribution. That cannot build a road circuit.

Two processes. Do not mix them up.

A. Create the money

Cabinet, the state budget, an election letter, or a clause in the MotoGP deal. The Premier, the Treasurer, the sport minister and tourism decide. This is how football got an $8 million fund. There is no form for this.

B. Draw the money down

A competitive grant once a pot already exists. You fill in SmartyGrants with a lease, plans, quotes and letters. This is how Playford clubs got changerooms. It is not how a new loop gets invented.

This project needs A first. B comes after there is a community trust, a real lease, and drawings.

The Bend myth — get this right or you lose the room

People say “government gave The Bend $30 million”. That figure is the private dragway project. Published government help was in the order of $7.5 million federal plus $7.5–10.5 million state for power, water and access, and later $2 million from the Regional Growth Fund toward the dragway. Getting this wrong destroys credibility.

The useful lesson is the pattern: government paid for enabling works. Someone else paid for the expensive dirt. A junior loop at Virginia should be sold the same way — services, access, amenities, a deed — not “give us a SuperDrome”.

Why Playford has to be in the photo

Thriving Suburbs wants a council or an incorporated not-for-profit. CRSFP scoring asks whether the project is in a local strategy. Playford already funds ovals that are named in its 2023 Sports Facility Strategy. The raceway is not in that strategy today. Until it is named — or a council resolution does the same job — the local path that worked for Dwight Reserve is closed.

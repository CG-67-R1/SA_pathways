Adelaide International Raceway

The land is already a raceway. The first build is a new loop on the car park.

Port Wakefield Road, Virginia SA 5120. Entrance off Brown Road. About 26 kilometres north of Adelaide. Next door to Speedway City.

CouncilCity of Playford
State seatTaylor — Nick Champion MP, who is also Planning and Housing minister
Federal seatSpence — Matt Burnell MP
OwnerBob Jane group. This is private land, not a council reserve.
Built1972. Full circuit 2.41 km, short 1.77 km, Super Bowl 0.80 km, drag strip
Last racingCircuit 1999. Drag 2018. Bikes left earlier because of the concrete wall.

Why the outside car park

The old Super Bowl has a concrete wall and almost no runoff for about 1.3 kilometres. Housing is closer now. A minister can fund a new, quiet, junior-safe loop. They will struggle to fund a revival of the wall.

The outside paddock / car park is the locked first product: the main paddock east of the main straight, behind the tower. Often the flattest sealed pad. Fastest to convert if the pavement is thick enough.

There is unused ground inside the Super Bowl as well. That is a fallback only. It is not the plan.

If you use the car park, you must replace the parking. Council and the local member will ask where event cars go. If that answer is missing, the project dies as “you removed the car park”.

What “small short circuit” means

  • 800 metres to 1.2 kilometres, sealed
  • About 6–8 metres wide, with runoff for 50–300 cc junior bikes
  • Low kerbs. No concrete death wall. Separate from the old Bowl
  • Daytime use and the existing 11 pm curfew
  • Clubrooms, toilets, medical and shade as the “community facility” in any grant — not just asphalt

Cost honesty, not a quote: if the pad only needs overlay, runoff, fence and amenities, think high six figures to about $1.5 million. If you need new pavement, drainage, lighting and a small building, think $1.5–4 million. A full national rebuild of the old circuit is a different fight.

Housing is the clock

Playford’s Virginia growth area runs toward Port Wakefield Road. Nearby housing has already been approved with a noise barrier because of this raceway. The State already admits the track is a noise source. The question is whether it stays a lawful community motorsport use, or becomes a land bank.

The Planning Minister who can protect it is the same local member who has been rezoning Virginia for housing. That conversation has to be “small, quiet, already-cleared junior academy” — not “save motorsport from your houses”.

The lease comes before any grant

A new circuit on private land is a permanent improvement. If the lease is short, the community has built the owner a housing-adjacent asset. Before anyone mentions a grant:

  • 15–21 years, plus an option
  • Permitted use: motorcycle and club racing, junior academy, training, community events
  • Published community-access days at a capped hire rate
  • Grant-funded works stay with the community, or are protected if the land is sold
  • No carving the loop parcel off for houses during the term

The official hire contact is already on the venue website. Do not use it unless the project lead says so.

Planning, in one paragraph

Using the old raceway is existing use since 1972. Pouring a new loop on the car park is almost certainly new development. You will need planning consent even if the land has always been motorsport. The exact zone name on the state planning map has not been pulled yet — do not tell a minister a zone name until that screenshot exists.

How other codes got large public money

They did not get lucky on a grant form. They arrived with a plan, a partner and a hook.

Every big cheque in South Australian sport followed one of a few recipes. Motorcycle road racing has the event (MotoGP) and almost none of the rest. That is constructible. It is also homework. See what we must do.

Football — the template

The State bought AFL Gather Round. The event deal itself promised a community leftover. SANFL already had a 10-year facilities plan and participant numbers a minister could repeat. The South Australian Football Facilities Fund was $8 million, then another $8 million when the event was locked on. Clubs and councils then applied for pieces of it. Official guidelines call it a legacy of the event.

Copy the fund, not the oval. Motorcycle racing has a stronger claim on MotoGP than football had on Gather Round. It does not have SANFL. The fund can still be asked for as a Premier / tourism / sport-minister product, with Playford and a clean trust as the first capital project.

Netball — the stadium

Mile End is being rebuilt for about $92 million. The State already owned the site and Netball SA was the sitting tenant, with 35,000 regular players. Planning started as an $18 million idea in 2021. It grew. Stage 1 outdoor courts were $6 million federal plus $6 million state — a first stage you can open and photograph. The Premier announced the extra $80 million at a Thunderbirds game. The building was widened to other indoor sports so more people could own it.

A car-park loop will never be Mile End. Steal the staging and the matching-funds shape, not the price tag.

Soccer — the northern headquarters

Football SA lodged a business case in 2017. In 2018 the Treasurer put $19 million into a $26 million State Centre of Football at Gepps Cross, inside a $30 million State Sports Park bundle (velodrome, BMX, hockey in the same announcement). Federal Labor later pledged $7.4 million to finish it. The land was already public. Opening day was 2022 — about five years from the business case.

Closest built-form analog: a new dedicated facility in Adelaide’s north, sold as juniors and women. The difference that kills a lazy copy: AIR is private land. Public money therefore needs a long community lease and a deed.

Tennis and golf — tourism venues

Memorial Drive Stage 2 was $44 million in a state budget because tourism already owned the Adelaide International. The North Adelaide golf course is a $45 million taxpayer upgrade, originally to keep LIV Golf, now pointed at the Australian Opens. That proves the State will write eight figures when the Premier wants the event. It also proves tying capital to one promoter is risky. SATC will not asphalt a Virginia car park. They can bless the word “legacy”.

Other circuits, elsewhere

Collie in Western Australia was funded as a coal-town jobs and tourism story. Hidden Valley in the Territory is, in substance, a government tourism asset. Broadford in Victoria got $50,000 of feasibility money because the state motorcycle body and the shire were already invested. The Bend, as above, was enabling works plus private capital. None of them won a $750,000 changeroom grant and accidentally became a circuit.

A junior motorcycle pathway at Adelaide International Raceway

A grassroots leftover of Adelaide MotoGP 2027–2032. Independent briefing, 4 October 2026. A dedicated working group is already carrying this.

Please do not send this to ministers, council or the landowner unless the project lead has asked you to. Use your browser’s print function if you want a paper copy.

The site

Adelaide International Raceway, Port Wakefield Road (entrance Brown Road), Virginia SA 5120. City of Playford. About 31 km north of Adelaide. Built 1972. Owner: Bob Jane group. A community not-for-profit would seek a long lease. First build: a new small short circuit on the south-western spectator car park (about 770 m × 270 m; 800 m–1.2 km junior-safe loop), not a rebuild of the 1972 Super Bowl wall. More than 1,000 car parks plus the pits remain. A car-club proposal can stay on the old circuit.

The problem

Adelaide will host MotoGP from 2027 to 2032. The State is investing in the order of $96 million in the street circuit and cites around $200 million a year in economic benefit. There is no published junior or club pathway attached to that spend.

The only two operating sealed circuits — The Bend and Mallala — are under one private owner. State titles at The Bend in late August 2026 drew 42 entries (32 solos). Club hire is about $35,000 plus about $4,500 a day for medical; a meeting that size loses about $20,000. AIR has had no sanctioned circuit racing since 1999. Virginia housing is expanding to Port Wakefield Road. Nearby lots have already been approved with a noise barrier because of this raceway. If the use is not locked, the pathway dies.

The precedent

After AFL Gather Round, the State, SANFL and the AFL created an $8 million Football Facilities Fund, then another $8 million. Official guidelines call it a legacy of the event. Motorcycle racing is the sport MotoGP actually is. It has not been offered the same deal.

The request

  1. Protect AIR’s motorsport and community-recreation use on the Virginia growth front.
  2. Create an $8 million, three-year Motorcycle Pathway and Facilities Fund as a formal leftover of Adelaide MotoGP.
  3. Support Playford and a community trust to apply for federal Thriving Suburbs ($250,000–$15 million) for a junior-safe academy — not a private upgrade without a deed.
  4. Make published community and junior days, the curfew, and grant works that survive a sale, conditions of any public money.

Who would run it

An independent South Australian not-for-profit, led by Brian Rosser, with Phoenix, Café Racers, Historic, Atujara and the wider Project Apex clubs at the table. Not Motorcycling SA as lead. Playford as grant partner. Insurance for coaching and come-and-try arranged commercially. Motorcycling Australia is not a partner in this version. ACUSA Park was already studied (March 2026) and is closed.

A fact that must stay correct

When The Bend was built, government contributed in the order of $7.5 million each from Canberra and Adelaide for services and access, plus $2 million later toward the dragway. Government did not write a $30 million cheque for the circuit.
